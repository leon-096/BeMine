A letter for you

My Dearest Elena,

I have been trying to find the right words for you since the day we met, and I think that may be the whole problem — there are a hundred right ways to begin, and every single one of them begins with you.

You are the sort of person who can make an ordinary Tuesday feel like something worth remembering. You laugh at my terrible jokes, you stay for the serious ones, and somehow you do both without ever once making me feel small. I do not think you have any idea what that does to a person.

I have learned that I do not want the loud, enormous moments. I want the quiet ones — the long drives with the windows down, the film we finish even though we hated it, the way you steal the blanket and then pretend you did not. I would like to keep choosing those with you for a very long time.

So I wrote you a letter, and then a small poem, and then I built you this quiet little place to read them in. It is not much. But everything I have right now, I would rather hand to you than keep.

With all my heart, always yours, Your Name

And a little poem

In the quiet hours before the light, I find your name and hold it tight — no crown, no ring, no velvet rope, just this: my stubborn, steady hope.

You turned a stranger into a friend, and then into the sweetest thing. I never went looking for a sign, it turned out you were mine.

I promise you the ordinary days: the same old road, a different phase. The same old song, but sung by two will never sound the way it used to.

If I could build a house from words, it would have its windows wide, a door that never locks for you, and one small light left on inside.

So here it is — my whole, small heart, beating like a bell that starts. Take my hand. Take my quiet years. Answer me with one word: yes.

Will you be mine?
